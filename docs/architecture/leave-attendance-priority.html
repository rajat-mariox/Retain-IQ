What's actually mandatory — a real-SaaS lens

These are the remaining Leave/Attendance items. Not everything on a roadmap is required to ship a credible product. Below, each is graded by how essential it is for a real HR/workforce SaaS — so you spend effort where it counts.

Must-have — a serious HR product looks incomplete without it Should-have — expected by mid-market, but you can launch without it Nice-to-have — differentiator / enterprise; build on demand

Must-have

Customers will notice these missing. Prioritise.

Attendance regularization MUST

Employee forgot to check in/out → files a correction → manager approves → attendance fixed. Without it, one missed punch = a wrong record the person can't fix.

Real SaaS: Every attendance product (Keka, Zoho, greytHR) has this. Note: you already have "attendance corrections" — this may be 80% done; worth auditing before building.

Supporting-document upload on leave MUST

Attach a medical certificate to sick leave, a proof to bereavement, etc. Approvers need evidence for certain leave types; auditors need it retained.

Real SaaS: Standard. You already have S3/MinIO + presigned uploads (screenshots) — the plumbing exists, so this is low effort, high expectation.

Should-have

Mid-market expects these, but a launch/MVP survives without them.

Attendance calendar + team availability SHOULD

A month grid of present/absent/leave/holiday per employee, and "who's available this week". You have the leave org-calendar; attendance isn't visualised the same way yet.

Real SaaS: Common and well-liked, but managers can live off the daily roster + leave calendar you already have.

Work schedule management SHOULD

Flexible / hybrid / remote / recurring shift patterns per employee or team. You have shifts + rotations; this is the richer scheduling layer on top.

Real SaaS: Matters a lot for shift-heavy orgs (retail, BPO), less for a standard 9–6 office. Depends on your target customer.

Overtime management SHOULD

Approve/reject accrued OT, mark billable, see trends. You already track & display OT (pending-approval) — the approval + reporting layer is what's missing.

Real SaaS: Important where OT is paid/billed. Since you track it already, closing the loop is moderate effort.

Scheduled reports SHOULD

Auto-email a weekly/monthly attendance or leave report to managers. You have report views + CSV export; this adds a scheduler + delivery.

Real SaaS: A convenience admins love, not a blocker. You have the report data already.

Nice-to-have / differentiator

Build these when a customer asks, or to stand out — not before.

Attendance intelligence scores NICE

Punctuality / consistency / risk scores per employee from attendance patterns. A differentiator, not table stakes.

Real SaaS: Premium/AI-tier feature. Fits your "workforce intelligence" positioning later.

Geofencing / location verification NICE

Verify check-in happened at office/site coordinates. You already capture work-location; GPS enforcement is the add-on.

Real SaaS: Critical for field/hourly workforces, irrelevant for remote/office. Build only if your customers are field-based.

Workforce planning / capacity forecast NICE

Predict staffing gaps from leave + headcount + demand. Advanced analytics, enterprise-tier.

Real SaaS: A high-end differentiator (Workday territory). Far down the list.

If I had to pick the next 2: Supporting-document upload on leave (must-have, and your S3 plumbing already exists — cheap win) and Attendance regularization (must-have, and likely already ~80% done via your corrections feature — audit first, then finish).

Everything in Should is genuinely optional for now — pick based on your target customer (shift-heavy? → schedules + OT; office? → skip). Everything in Nice waits for a paying customer to ask.

The honest answer to "is it mandatory?": Only 2 of these are truly mandatory (document upload, regularization). The rest are "expected eventually" or "differentiators". You already shipped the hard parts — leave lifecycle, half-day, approval center, conflict detection, idle auto-checkout. You're past MVP on Leave/Attendance; what remains is polish + reach, not gaps that make the product look broken.
